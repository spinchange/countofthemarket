The Trend of the Market

S. & P. 500 Gains 1.2 Per Cent on the Week—Cumulative Advance-Decline Line 454 Issues Below July Peak—New Highs Exceed New Lows Fourteenth Week—The Week’s Count Less than Even

By the Editor

The S. & P. 500 finished the week at 7,575.39, up 1.23 per cent, 0.1 per cent below its high for the year of 7,580.06, set May 29. Underneath the average, the count of the market has gone the other way. The cumulative advance-decline line made its peak on July 2 at 6,988. In the one week since, it has given back 454 issues, and it now stands at 6,534.

The week’s own count was worse than even: 5,256 issues advanced and 5,710 declined. An average that rises while the count falls is being carried by fewer stocks.

New
Highs

New highs have outnumbered new lows in each of the last fourteen weeks. This week the exchanges recorded 1,055 new highs against 643 new lows. The ten-week average of issues advancing is 52.5 per cent, down from 53.1 a week ago.

Why We Call It the Count of the Market

A Note on Col. Leonard P. Ayres, 1879–1946, and the Daily Tally He Began

Leonard Ayres served as chief statistician of the Army in the First World War. He later became vice president and economist of the Cleveland Trust Company, where he wrote its monthly Business Bulletin. With his assistant, James F. Hughes, he kept a daily tally of how many stocks rose and how many fell. He called it the count of the market.

“I think this will make an interesting stock market statistic.”AYRES TO HUGHES, AS HUGHES RECALLED IT IN 1951

The average tells you how far the market moved. The count tells you how many stocks went along. This publication keeps that count every Friday, in the manner Ayres began it.
